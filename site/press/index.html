For the press.

Musicians describe music in pictures: “make it warmer”, “the bit that goes da-da-DUM”. Software wants numbers. For years AJ Smith, a musician and computer scientist, has been the person in the middle, turning friends’ ideas into notes, plugins and knob positions. Overdub puts an AI agent in that chair. It is a music studio in the browser: you hum, tap, play or say a take, and your agent plays over it with notes, settings, and instruments and effects it writes as code, which the studio tests before anyone hears them. Takes go into the song in time, after a count-in, and any knob can be drawn or recorded as automation. Agents can’t hear, so the studio measures the mix for them. Every edit is signed, warm for you and cool for the agent, and the agent’s can be undone without touching yours. Overdub never generates audio.

Long 261 words

Overdub is a music studio in the browser, built for a musician and their AI agents to make music together.

Musicians describe music in pictures: “make it warmer”, “the bit that goes da-da-DUM”. Software wants numbers. Between the two there has usually been a person who translates. Overdub’s maker, AJ Smith, a musician and computer scientist, has been that person for years. Overdub puts an AI agent in that chair and keeps the musician’s hands on the song.

There are four ways in. Hum, and pitch tracking writes the notes. Tap, and the groove lands on a drum grid. Play a guitar, a MIDI keyboard or the computer keys. Press record first and the take lands in the song after a count-in, every pass kept. Or say what you want, and the agent changes what is already there and shows exactly what it moved. Ask for an effect that doesn’t exist and the agent writes it as code; the studio tests it before it plays, then gives it a face with real knobs.

Agents can’t hear, so the studio measures for them, reporting loudness, peaks, spectrum and key in musicians’ terms. Every edit records who made it, so the agent’s work can be undone without touching the musician’s.

Overdub never generates audio: everything is notes, parameters and code. It grows out of the Guitar Studio in AJ’s browser punk-song maker Claw’d-o-Matic, whose 101 pedals, 27 amps and 156 rigs it includes. Outside agents such as Claude Code connect over MCP. Overdub is an early prototype, free, and runs in your browser.

Name
Overdub. Overdub Studio on first mention or where it needs to be found. Sentence case in prose; “overdub” in lower case in the wordmark. Never “OverDub”.
Lines
Play over each other. A studio for you and your agents.
What it is
A music studio (a DAW) in the browser, built for a musician and their AI agents.
Status
Early prototype, live since 30 September 2026. There are no user numbers to report yet.
Web
overdubstudio.com (this site) and overdubstudio.com/app/ (the studio)
Price
Free. A demo agent runs in the studio; a musician’s own Claude (Claude Code, on their Claude plan) is the live one. The studio keeps no API key.
Platform
Any current browser (Chrome, Edge, Safari, Firefox), on a computer or a phone. No install, no account. Web MIDI needs Chrome, Edge or Firefox.
Made by
AJ Smith
Built-in instruments and effects
23, all synthesized in the page: 13 instruments and 10 effects, named after things in a studio (Patch Bay, Capstan, Stairwell, Echo Reel…).
From the Guitar Studio
101 pedals, 27 amps, 16 cabinets, 5 mics and 156 rigs, from AJ’s Claw’d-o-Matic, each rig leveled by measurement.
Agent tools
38, shared by the agent in the studio and by outside agents over MCP.
Agent-built devices
13 ship with it: 10 on the house shelf in the device library and 3 in Night Shift, each carrying the request that asked for it.
Ways in
Hum (pitch tracking), tap (to a drum grid), guitar or bass through an audio interface, MIDI keyboards, the computer keyboard, and words to the agent.
Recording
Into the song from a start marker, after a count-in of one or two bars, with a metronome you can see. Round a loop, drum passes layer into one clip and other passes stack as takes.
Automation
Lanes for level, pan and any knob: drawn, recorded from a knob as it turns, or written by the agent. They play the same live, in an export and in a render.
Demo songs
10, each in a different genre, from lo-fi to bossa nova. Each has a part signed by Claude, written ahead of time like the rest.
What it measures
Integrated loudness (LUFS), true peak, crest, band energy, brightness, stereo width, onsets, key and a spectrogram.
Agents
Claude in the studio (Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 or Haiku 4.5, with the musician’s key); Claude Code and other MCP clients from outside, connected to a local copy of the studio.
Generated audio
None. Every sound is synthesized from notes, parameters and code.
Your song
Saved in your browser. No account.
Privacy
No account. On the live site, anonymous counts only (page views, and in the studio opens, plays, agent messages, devices, exports, shares, hums and keeps): no cookies, no ids, never the song. Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control turn them off. The in-studio agent talks straight to Anthropic with the musician’s key.
Code
Plain JavaScript modules: no build step, no dependencies.

Counts come from the code as it runs today, not from a plan.

The Weave and the wordmark.

Two strands cross twice. At the first crossing the warm one (you) is on top; at the second, the cool one (your agent) is. All four files are vectors.

Colours

  • Graphite#141210
  • Cream#f4ead6
  • Leader green#d9f36a
  • Grease pencil#f1dc8a
  • Warm: a person#ffa043
  • Cool: an agent#4cc3ff

Please don’t

  • Recolour the strands. Warm is always the person, cool is always the agent.
  • Swap which strand is on top at the first crossing, add a third, or straighten the wobble.
  • Outline it, add a shadow, or set it on a busy photo.

Type: Archivo at its widest, ExtraBold Italic, for display; Atkinson Hyperlegible Next and Mono for everything else. All free on Google Fonts.
