Overdub is a music studio for you and your agents. You lay down a take: hum it, tap it, play it or say it. Your agent plays over it, you play over that, and every take is signed: warm for you, cool for your agent.

“Warmer” means something a little different to everyone who says it. Your agent asks which one you mean, and always shows the exact change it made.

Somebody has to translate. For years that has been Overdub’s maker, AJ Smith, a musician and computer scientist: the friend who hears an idea and turns it into notes, plugins and knob positions. It is slow, and good ideas fade on the way.

Overdub puts an agent in that chair. You keep your hands on the song, and the agent plays on it, in your words and the DAW’s numbers.

Four ways to lay down take one.

Overdub records first and tidies later. Everything you hum, tap or play is kept in the Takes list, so the idea you hum at 2 a.m. is still there in the morning.

Press R and the take goes into the song.

Select a track and press R. The bar before the start marker plays with the click while a numeral over the track counts 4 3 2 1, then whatever you play, tap or hum lands on that track at the bars where you played it. Space stops and keeps the take; ⌘Z takes it back out.

Marker on bar 5, loop on 5 and 64567

  1. Count in, then watch the ball

    One bar of count-in, two, or none (⇧K). Beside the position a square lands on each beat as you hear it, so you can see the next one coming with the sound off. It goes red while you record.

    4321

  2. Drums, round the loop

    The loop is the punch range. On a drum track each pass layers into one clip: kick and snare the first time round, hats the second, every hit where you played it.

    Drums2 passesyou

  3. Keys, take after take

    On keys, a hum or a guitar, each pass is a new take in one folder, and ⌘↑ ⌘↓ pick the one that plays. What was there before is muted under the take, on those bars and nowhere else.

    Keys3 takesyou
  • R

    With the song already playing, R waits for the next bar line and counts the beats left in this one. Tempo, meter and the loop wait until the take stops.

  • Takes

    Played something without recording? It’s in Sketch’s Takes list, and Put it in the song (⇧R for the newest) places it at the beats where you played it if the song was running, or at the marker.

  • Sketch

    Its record strip shows a beat ruler with each tap where it lands, and switches each pass between Layer and New take, per track.

Describe a sound and your agent writes the device.

Ask for an instrument or an effect that doesn’t exist yet. The agent writes it as a small piece of audio code. The studio checks it before you hear it: it has to compile, never output NaN, stay under control at the ends of its knobs and never leave a note stuck, and its loudness is measured against what went in. Then it gets a face with real knobs, and it’s yours.

An agent can’t hear your mix, so the studio measures it.

Overdub renders the mix offline, through the same signal path you hear, and measures it: loudness, peaks, spectrum, key. The agent checks its own work before it asks for your ears, then tells you what it found the way an engineer would: louder than your reference, a bit thick in the low mids.

This is how the Guitar Studio’s presets were leveled: every one within a decibel of −14 LUFS (leads sit a touch hotter), true peak at or below −1 dBFS.

Every take is signed.

Warm is you and cool is your agent. Every note, clip and knob turn records who made it and why, so you can see what changed and take back the agent’s last idea without losing yours. Ask for options and you get two to four takes to audition side by side.

    Draw the fade, or record the knob as you turn it.

    Any knob, fader or pan can move over the song. Its moves live in a lane under its track, and the lanes play the same in the studio, in an export and in the render an agent measures.

    Pad and its lanes1234

    1. Draw it

      E opens a track’s lanes: Level, Pan or any knob. Click to add a point and drag it, or select some bars and pick a shape: Ramp up, Ramp down, Swell, Dip, Hold here or Pulse.

      Padyou

    2. Ask for it

      “Fade the pad in over bars 1–4” is one call. The agent writes the lane, then renders and measures the bars at each end, and corrects it once if the level moved the wrong way.

      Level, fade inClaude

    3. Record it

      Press R and turn a knob: what your hand does goes into its lane from where you touched it to where you let go, then the lane takes over again with a 200 ms glide.

      Keyhole cutoffyou
    • auto

      A knob with a lane turns by itself and says so. Turn it yourself and it says held until you give it Back to the lane.

    • Just playing

      Turn a knob while the song just plays and it stays where you left it. The toast says what moved, over which bars, and offers Write it into the lane, as if you had recorded it.

    Stop one part, or every sound at once.

    The start marker keeps you on the bars you’re working on, and anything that sounds can be stopped from the keyboard.

    • Space

      Plays from the start marker and stops back on it, so you can pick bar 4 and work a part bar by bar. ⇧Space plays on from where it stopped; Home puts the marker back on bar 1.

    • 0

      Mutes the selected clips, even while the song plays. A muted clip is printed in outline with its name struck through, and it plays nowhere: not in the song, an export or a render. Trim to the loop cuts a long take down to the loop’s bars.

    • ⇧Esc

      All off, from anywhere, even a text field: every note, reverb and delay tail, preview and click, whoever started it.

      −90 dBFS within 50 ms
    • Stop

      Lets every note go, the ones already on their way to an instrument too. Reverbs and delays ring on for 2 seconds, as on a desk, then fade. With the loop off, the song stops by itself a bar after its last note.

      every demo song: −60 dBFS 3.5 s after Stop

    Open all the way down.

    The song is a document.

    One JSON file holds the whole song. The buttons, the agent in the studio and agents outside it all change it the same way: small typed edits you can read, undo and replay.

  "notes": "A3@0:1 C4@1:1 E4@2:2" }

    Devices are code.

    Instruments and effects are small DSP kernels that run in the audio thread with no clock and seeded randomness. Same input, same output, every time. That rule is for determinism; it is not a security sandbox. Faces are drawn from their knobs, so nobody writes UI to make a pedal.

    process(L, R, n, p) {
  for (let i = 0; i < n; i++)
    L[i] = lp.lp(L[i]);
}

    • Share

      A link carries the song and every name on it.

      The whole song rides in the link, so Overdub’s servers never see it (whatever you paste it into does; recordings stay home). Whoever opens it listens first; Make it yours keeps a copy that says where it came from, every part still signed by whoever played it. A link can carry devices someone wrote, which are code: they stay off until you press Play them, so play them for people you trust.

    • Transforms

      16 transforms, for both of you.

      Humanize, strum, arpeggiate, invert, chords from a melody, fill the gap: in the piano roll’s Transform menu, signed by you. The agent has them too, and when one would rewrite your notes it offers takes instead.
